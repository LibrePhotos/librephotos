1.4.0: Edits written to your files, editing many photos at once, search filters

October 17, 2026 · 18 min read
Niaz Faridani-Rad
Maintainer of LibrePhotos

This release makes the edits you make in LibrePhotos reach your files, lets you change the date and place of many photos at once, adds filters to search, and brings album collections and an archive. Updating gets easier too: the work an update leaves now runs from the app. It also fixes a long list of bugs in scans, dates, faces, places and stacks, and a security problem.

Who should update: Everyone. If other people have an account on your LibrePhotos, update right away: this release fixes a security problem.

What's new for you:

  • With Write edits to files on, your tags, captions, locations and rotations now go into your files too, next to ratings, dates and face tags. Write now under Write existing edits to files writes the edits you made before. See What LibrePhotos writes.
  • Select photos and choose Change date and time… or Set location… to fix a camera with a wrong clock, or place a whole trip at once. See Changing the date of many photos.
  • Click Filters on the search results to narrow them by people, camera, date, place, rating or file type. Search also ignores accents now: "Zurich" finds "Zürich". See Search filters.
  • Group your albums into Collections, and use Archive to take photos off the timeline while they stay in your albums. See Collections and Archive.
  • Admins: a Finish the upgrade card in the Admin Area runs the work an update leaves and shows how far it got. See The Finish the upgrade card.

Before you update: Make a backup as usual. The Windows app and the single container with its built-in database now also copy the database by themselves before every database update. See Automatic backups.

After you update:

  • Nothing to do for most of it. The first start takes longer than usual, because LibrePhotos repairs some data once, for example duplicate Nextcloud photos. Admins see the remaining work on the Finish the upgrade card under Account menu > Admin Area: it removes photo details from old thumbnails and reads shutter speeds again by itself. The first Scan after the update checks every file once, so it takes longer too.
  • Recommended for everyone: rescan your photos once. Go to Account menu > Library, then the arrow next to Scan > Rescan. This reads what LibrePhotos did not read before: dates, places and camera details from XMP sidecars, the date of MKV, WebM and phone videos, and Google Takeout files. It is done when the job in Account menu > My Jobs has finished. If you skip it, those details only show up for photos that change.
  • Windows app, west of Greenwich (for example in the Americas): the rescan is required. Up to 1.3.0, the Windows app stored scan times wrongly there, so a normal Scan missed files you had edited.
  • Optional: rescan faces once if face detection ever failed on your server, or if other apps wrote face tags into your photos. Go to Account menu > Library and click Rescan in the Rescan Faces row. If you skip it, faces in those photos stay undetected.
  • If you use stacks: go to Photos > Organizing, open Stacks and click Detect Photo Stacks once. Bursts are grouped better now, and old stacks stay as they were until you do this.
  • If you write face tags to files: face tags of rotated photos (most phone portraits) were written to the wrong place. Go to Account menu > Settings > Metadata Options and click Write now under Write existing edits to files to write them again.
  • Admins: settings, log file and Docker changes
    New site settings: Missing photos limit, Scan libraries automatically, Delete photos from the trash after (off by default) and a Features card. New environment variables: STUCK_JOB_HOURS, SIDECAR_PORT_<NAME>, NEXTCLOUD_CA_BUNDLE, FEATURE_VIDEO_PREVIEWS and the thumbnail and video preview sizes; remove DEFAULT_FAVORITE_MIN_RATING from your .env, it is no longer read (see Environment variables). The log file is now librephotos.log: point log shippers and fail2ban filters at the new name (details). The single container Compose file moved to deploy/compose/docker-compose.unified.yml, reads librephotos.env and mounts the database at /var/lib/postgresql: back up the dbcursor folder in your data folder first. The generated secret key moves to protected_media/secret.key; copy it back before you go back to 1.3.0 or older, or everyone has to log in again (details). Scripts that use the API: list endpoints page by 100 with one envelope, errors have one shape, and album ids are numbers everywhere.

Upstream:

🚀 LibrePhotos: Your Edits, Written to Your Files

With Write edits to files set to sidecar or media file, LibrePhotos now writes your tags (as keywords), captions, locations and rotations back to the photo, like ratings, dates and face tags before. The new Write existing edits to files row in Settings > Metadata Options writes the edits you made before you turned it on, as a job with progress and Cancel (#2287). Edits go into the sidecar that already exists (IMG.jpg.xmp from digiKam or darktable) instead of a second one, deleting a face also removes its region from the file, and when ExifTool could not write an edit, an orange toast says so instead of a green "done". Reading got the same care: the scan imports captions, title, creator, copyright and more keywords from IPTC, EXIF and XMP, takes dates, GPS, camera and lens from sidecars and from the sidecars of RAW and video variants, reads Google Takeout JSON files, and recognises LibrePhotos' own writes, so a bulk write no longer turns the next scan into a full rescan. The info panel shows camera, lens, exposure, megapixels and coordinates, owners can open an All metadata view with every tag of the file and its sidecars, and zip downloads include the XMP sidecars. With writing Off (the default), LibrePhotos now really leaves your files alone: a scan never writes to them.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Edit Many Photos at Once

Select photos and choose Change date and time… to shift them all by the same amount (a camera with a wrong clock, or one left on summer time) or give them one date (#2278), or Set location… to place them all at once, optionally copying the position of a photo taken around the same time (#2279). A photo's location can also be removed now. Changed date rules can be applied to the photos already in the library without a full rescan, captions can be generated for the whole library as one job, the files of selected photos can be rescanned and faces detected for one folder or a selection, and photos can be moved with all their files to another folder of the library. An authenticated scan-files API lets an outside process tell LibrePhotos about new files.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Collections, Archive and Groups of People

Albums can be grouped into collections such as Travel or Family, and an album can be in several (#1009). You can upload straight into one of your albums and create an album before it has photos. The new Archive takes photos off the timeline and Memories while they stay in albums, people, places and search (#545), and Photos not in an album shows what is left to sort. Album lists sort by name, count or date, come back to the same place on Back, and events can get a cover you pick. People can be put in groups ("Family", "Work") to filter the People page and the face dashboard by, two people can be merged into one, deleted faces can be restored from a new Deleted tab, and the face dashboard gets a Confirm button on each face and a grid of people with their cover faces.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Search Filters

The search results page has a Filters button: people (photos with all of them together, #329), camera, lens, ISO, aperture, focal length, rating, date taken, location and file type, including "Has a RAW file" (#2259). Active filters show as chips you can remove one by one, and they live in the URL, so a link keeps them and Back undoes the last change. You can also search by filters alone, without any words, through Filter photos in the command palette. Search now ignores accents and letter case in both directions: Zurich finds "Zürich", and Lodz finds "Łódź".

🚀 LibrePhotos: Safer Updates and Scans

The work an update leaves is no longer only in the release notes: a Finish the upgrade card at the top of the Admin Area runs the follow-up tasks and shows their progress, with a dot on the avatar while anything is left (#2322). While the backend updates its database or starts, the web app shows LibrePhotos is updating instead of empty pages, and a small start-up page answers on the backend port from the first second. The Windows app and the single container with SQLite copy the database before every migration, and an older release refuses to start on a database a newer one has migrated. Scans got safer too: a scan of an unmounted or empty library no longer marks every photo missing, an unreadable folder is skipped instead of failing the whole scan, Cancel really stops a job's work, and a long first scan is no longer failed after 24 hours. Admins can also have LibrePhotos scan every library on a schedule from Site settings, with no host cron job (#235).

🚀 LibrePhotos: Security Fix

A logged-in user could change the hash of one of their own photos through the photo API. The hash names the photo's thumbnail files, so rotating the photo afterwards wrote and deleted .webp and .mp4 files outside the thumbnail folders, or replaced another user's thumbnails (GHSA-f7vw-q3m9-4998). The hash is now set by the server only, and every thumbnail path refuses a hash that is not a plain file name. Every API view now also declares who may call it, the token endpoints and failed Basic authentication attempts are rate limited, and the generated secret key is no longer kept in the logs folder. Thanks to shaoyuuuuu for the responsible disclosure.

1.3.0: Timeline filter, videos that play everywhere, faster search

October 11, 2026 · 9 min read
Niaz Faridani-Rad
Maintainer of LibrePhotos

This release adds a timeline filter that keeps screenshots and documents out of the way, makes videos play in every browser, makes search, the API and scans a lot faster on less memory, and fixes a set of reported security issues, including two code execution bugs.

Who should update: Everyone. If other people use your LibrePhotos, or you share public links, update right away: this release fixes security problems.

What's new for you:

  • Use the new Filter button on the timeline to hide screenshots and documents, or show only videos or favorites. Save as default keeps your choice.
  • Videos that did not play in your browser before now play, for example iPhone videos in Firefox.
  • If a photo is marked as a screenshot or document by mistake, fix it with the badge in the photo details.
  • Search, scans and the app are faster and need less memory.

After you update:

  • Nothing to do for search and tags. After the update, LibrePhotos reads every photo again in the background so that tags and search use the new model. Search finds more photos while this runs, and the Things albums fill up again. You can follow it under Account menu > My Jobs.
  • Admins: remove photo details from old thumbnails once. Recommended for everyone, required if you use public links. Thumbnails made before 1.3.0 still contain the location and camera details of the photo, and public links show those thumbnails. One cleanup removes that data. Your photos and the thumbnails' pictures stay as they are, and running it twice does no harm.
    • Windows app: open the folder that contains librephotos.exe. Click the address bar of the folder window, type cmd and press Enter. In the black window, paste start /wait librephotos.exe manage strip_thumbnail_metadata and press Enter. If you start LibrePhotos with --data-dir, put the same --data-dir <folder> right after librephotos.exe. It is done when the window shows the prompt again.
    • Docker: in the folder with your docker-compose.yml, run docker compose exec backend python manage.py strip_thumbnail_metadata. Single container: docker exec librephotos python manage.py strip_thumbnail_metadata.
    • More detail: After upgrading from 1.2.1 or older.
  • Kubernetes
    The manifests in deploy/k8s now use 1.3.0 and PostgreSQL 16 instead of 2023w31 and PostgreSQL 13. Read the Kubernetes README before you apply them to an existing install.

Upstream:

🚀 LibrePhotos: One Open Model for Tags and Search

Tags, semantic search, and similar photos now all come from OpenCLIP ViT-B/32, trained by LAION on DataComp-XL and released under the MIT licence. It replaces CLIP ViT-B/32 for search and the separate tagging model, so a scan runs one vision model per photo: tagging a photo produces its search embedding in the same pass. The separate clip_embeddings service is gone, and so are the model choices in the site settings, because there is nothing left to choose. Captions (LFM2.5-VL) and faces (InsightFace) are unchanged. See Semantic search.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Faster Search, API, and Scans on Less Memory

The Rust and TypeScript backend experiments were much faster than Django, and most of the difference turned out to be queries, app-layer work, and ML memory rather than the language. Every optimization that applies to Django is now in LibrePhotos. On a 50,000 photo library, text search takes about half the time (1.19 s down to 0.56 s), album lists, the date list, user details, site settings, and thumbnails answer faster with fewer queries, and duplicate detection finishes in 27 seconds instead of running for hours. The idle API server uses about half the memory and starts in 3 seconds, heavy libraries load only when they are needed, and a scan with machine learning committed 2.25 GB instead of 10.7 GB in our test.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Timeline Filter and Category Correction

The timeline has a Filter button: show photos, videos, or both, hide screenshots, hide documents, or show only favorites, and Save as default to make that your timeline (#2130). Select all acts on exactly what the timeline shows. When the automatic detection gets a photo wrong, fix its category in the lightbox (a badge says whether it was detected or set by you, with Undo) or mark a whole selection as photo, screenshot, or document from the selection menu. The sidebar is tidier too: Memories now appear as a strip on top of the timeline, the organizing pages moved into the Photos menu, and background jobs show as a progress ring around your avatar.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Videos That Play in Every Browser

Each video is now probed once during the scan, and its codec, pixel format, and colour transfer are stored. The web app uses that to ask the browser whether it can play the file, and converts only the videos it cannot play (for example HEVC in Firefox), instead of leaving "Always transcode videos" as the only way to play them (#477, #714). HDR videos get an HDR badge on their tiles and correctly tonemapped thumbnails, and a Probe Videos job fills in videos scanned before this release. (Implemented by dotanm)

🚀 LibrePhotos: Security Fixes

This release fixes several reported vulnerabilities, so please upgrade. A person name containing a line break could inject options into ExifTool when face tags were written to files, which let a logged-in user run code on the server (GHSA-x465-w9j9-42rp). ExifTool tag names in a user's own date and burst detection rules could do the same in the exif service (GHSA-cgq8-jvp9-r3j7). On the unified image and the Windows standalone build, a crafted /media/ URL could read files outside the media folder (GHSA-cf3f-2fmg-mjj6). Thumbnails kept the original photo's GPS position and camera details, so public links leaked the location even with "Share location information" off (GHSA-j84j-mc98-7wq3). Nextcloud server addresses are now validated on the host the connection really uses, which closes a bypass of the check added in 1.2.0 (GHSA-xq77-cj4c-42qr). The advisory for the album and select_all owner scope fix that shipped in 1.2.0 is published too (GHSA-phvg-g65q-rhq3). Thanks to wk-cs, Seven11Eleven, and dotanm for the responsible disclosure.

1.2.1: SQLite databases repaired

September 29, 2026 · 3 min read
Niaz Faridani-Rad
Maintainer of LibrePhotos

This point release repairs SQLite databases. SQLite is the database of the unified Docker image and of the standalone Windows build, so if you run either of them, please upgrade. PostgreSQL installs are not affected by the repair.

Before you upgrade (SQLite only): two one-time migrations rebuild some tables. They copy each table before dropping the old one, so make sure you have free disk space of roughly the size of your database. On a test library with 1.6 million album entries and 160,000 faces they took about 30 seconds, and the database file grew by about 650 MB; SQLite reuses that space for later writes, and VACUUM hands it back to the file system. If your library already had photos when you upgraded past the switch to UUID photo IDs in early 2026, run "Rescan All Photos" (Settings, Library) after upgrading so camera, lens, and image size details are read from your files again (see below).

Upstream:

🚀 LibrePhotos: SQLite Databases Repaired

When photos moved from content hashes to UUID primary keys in early 2026, the SQLite version of that migration stored every existing photo's new ID with dashes, while Django stores and looks up UUIDs on SQLite without them. Those photos kept showing up in lists, but anything that looks a photo up by its ID missed them: their faces, thumbnails, captions, and metadata were not found, adding one to a new album left the album empty, and tagging or sharing one failed with "FOREIGN KEY constraint failed". The same migration also rebuilt twelve tables (album contents, faces, thumbnails, captions, search, files, and sharing) without their foreign keys and other constraints, on every SQLite install, fresh ones included. This release rewrites the stored IDs into the form Django reads, removes entries that pointed at photos, albums, or people that no longer exist, and rebuilds those tables with their foreign keys, so SQLite now enforces what PostgreSQL always did (#2121).

On the affected installs, the migration that moved camera, lens, and image size details into their own table also skipped the old photos, and the old columns were removed after it. "Rescan All Photos" reads those details from your files again.

1.2.0: A Windows app, Memories, and faster scans

September 29, 2026 · 10 min read
Niaz Faridani-Rad
Maintainer of LibrePhotos

This release brings a standalone Windows build that needs no Docker, moves every machine learning model to ONNX Runtime and drops PyTorch, brings Memories, makes scanning and the web app a lot faster, and closes a long list of security issues: all multi-user instances should upgrade. Here is an overview of all the new features, improvements, and bug fixes since the last release.

Before you upgrade: the first 100 database migrations are now combined into one. Installs on 2026w10 or later (including every 1.x release) upgrade directly as usual. Installs on an older release must first run 1.1.0 once so its migrations complete, then upgrade to this release. If you skip that step, the migration stops with a hint that names the upgrade path. See Upgrading from a release older than 2026w10.

Upstream:

🚀 LibrePhotos: Standalone Windows Build, No Docker Needed

LibrePhotos now runs on a plain Windows PC: download librephotos-windows-x64.zip from the assets of this release, unzip it anywhere, and double-click librephotos.exe. That one program is the web server, the web app, the background workers, and every machine learning service, with ExifTool and ffmpeg bundled next to it. There is nothing else to install: no Python, no Docker, no database server. The database is SQLite in %LOCALAPPDATA%\LibrePhotos, your photos stay where they are, and LibrePhotos lives in the notification area with an icon to open it, open the data or log folder, and quit (which stops everything it started). Your browser opens on the first-time setup, and the models download on the first scan. It is meant for one person's library on their own PC; for a shared server or PostgreSQL, keep using one of the Docker deployments. See the Windows standalone guide for options such as --data-dir and --photos.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Every ML Model on ONNX Runtime, PyTorch Removed

Tagging, captioning, semantic search, and face recognition now all run on ONNX Runtime. PyTorch, sentence-transformers, transformers, and llama.cpp are gone from the images, which makes them much smaller and faster to build. Default tagging now uses MobileCLIP-S2 zero-shot over the shared tag vocabulary instead of Places365, semantic search keeps the same CLIP ViT-B/32 weights in ONNX form, and captioning is a single small vision-language model (LFM2.5-VL-450M) that is always available and is prompted with the people and places in the photo, so captions can name who is in the picture and where it was taken. This replaces the BLIP captioner (broken for about a year), im2txt, Moondream, and the optional Mistral caption polish. The GPU image now actually runs the models on the GPU, ML services only start when their feature flag is on, and idle services unload their models to free memory.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Memories

A new Memories page shows the photos you took on this day in earlier years (#844). There is one tile per year with a cover, the place, and how many photos it holds; clicking a tile plays that year as a slideshow, "Play all" plays every year in a row, and a gallery switch shows the whole day across the years as one grid. Memories cover the anniversary plus or minus three days, roll over at your own midnight, and scale with the number of years in your library, not the number of photos. Nothing is injected into the timeline and nothing autoplays: if you do not want memories, you simply never open the page. (Implemented by dotanm)

🚀 LibrePhotos: Faster Scans and a Faster Web App

The scan pipeline was profiled end to end. The metadata service now reads every tag of a request in one ExifTool command instead of one per tag (about 390 ms down to about 30 ms per photo), thumbnails are decoded once per photo instead of three times, RAW files use the camera's embedded preview when it is good enough, every file is hashed once, face detection keeps the embeddings it already computed, and backend services are reached on 127.0.0.1 instead of localhost, which cost two seconds per request on some systems. In the browser, route code splitting now actually splits routes and translations load on demand, so the main JavaScript chunk shrinks from 3.68 MB to 829 KB. The photo grid moved to TanStack Virtual and drag and drop to dnd-kit.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Security Hardening

A thorough authorization review of the backend closed a series of cross-user leaks. User albums and the select_all download could be used to place another user's private photos in your own album and share them again; albums can now only vouch for their owner's photos. Three reported issues are fixed and published: a photo hash existence oracle (GHSA-hq2w-x39h-8wmp), the map API key being returned to anonymous callers (GHSA-6367-m327-c4pf), and other users' job records in /api/rqavailable/ (GHSA-975v-mx44-9jxq). On top of that: day albums, read-only album shares, folder paths, photo metadata lookups, chunked uploads, zip downloads, and hash-keyed photo lookups are scoped to the requesting user; public albums honour their location and time sharing settings; and password reset links can no longer be poisoned through the Host header. Thanks to Hama1cco for the responsible disclosure.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Duplicate Detection

A brand-new duplicate detection system using perceptual hashing! It detects exact copies via hash comparison and visual duplicates using a memory-efficient two-pass algorithm that works well even with very large collections. The API supports a batch_size parameter to fine-tune performance for your hardware.

🚀 LibrePhotos: Photo UUID Primary Key & Structured Metadata

The Photo model's primary key has been changed from image_hash to UUID, which is a massive internal refactor touching models, APIs, serializers, background tasks, and tests. Alongside this, a new structured PhotoMetadata model with edit history has been added, enabling richer metadata management.

🚀 Frontend: Public Album Sharing Options

Public album sharing now supports fine-grained per-album settings. You can control the visibility of location, camera info, timestamps, captions, and faces for each shared album. Default sharing preferences can be configured in user settings, and individual albums can override them.

🚀 Frontend: Slideshow Mode

A new slideshow mode has been added to the lightbox with a configurable slide interval. Sit back and enjoy your photos!

🚀 Frontend: Explore Page

The navigation dropdown has been replaced with a dedicated explore page, giving you a much better overview of your albums, people, places, things and events at a glance.

🚀 Frontend: Redesigned Album & Statistics Pages

The album page and all statistics/data visualization pages (face clusters, social graph, word clouds, timeline, place tree) have been completely redesigned with a fresh new look.

🚀 Frontend: Spotlight Search

A new spotlight search powered by Mantine replaces the old search bar, providing a faster and more intuitive search experience with keyboard navigation.

🚀 Frontend: Select All

You can now select all photos in a view for bulk operations like deleting, sharing, or adding to albums.

Development: 2026 - February

February 17, 2026 · One min read
Niaz Faridani-Rad
Maintainer of LibrePhotos

This release introduces a multi-model tagging system, giving you the choice of two different AI models for auto-tagging your photos.

Upstream:

  • 🚀 LibrePhotos/Frontend: Multi-Model Tagging System
    • You can now choose between two tagging models in Site Settings → Tagging Model:
      • Places365 — The classic scene recognition model (default)
      • SigLIP 2 — Google's latest vision-language model, running as ONNX. Uses zero-shot classification against a curated vocabulary of 900+ real-world photo tags (people, animals, nature, activities, scenes, weather, and more). Returns the top 10 most relevant tags.
    • Tags are stored per model — switching between models does not delete previously generated tags, and does not require rescanning your library.
    • The lightbox sidebar now shows tags with color-coded badges for each model (green for SigLIP 2, blue for Places365).
    • Things Albums are dynamically filtered to only show tags from the currently active tagging model.
    • Search results use tags from the selected model.
